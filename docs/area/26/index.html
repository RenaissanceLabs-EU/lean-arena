26 Real functions

Real functions

Can a curve be unbroken yet have no tangent anywhere, and when does integrating undo differentiating?

This area is the rigorous study of functions of one or several real variables: continuity, differentiation, integration, and the many ways in which these properties can hold or fail. It contains the theory behind calculus, together with finer topics such as monotone and convex functions, functions of bounded variation, classical inequalities, and generalized derivatives and integrals.

Arguments rest on the completeness of the real line and on careful estimates with inequalities, the method of epsilon and delta. A large part of the tradition consists of examples that mark the limits of theorems, such as a continuous function that has a derivative nowhere. The area is the base for measure theory, functional analysis, differential equations, approximation theory and optimization.

Central objects

Continuous function
A function whose values change by as little as desired when the input changes little enough.
Derivative
The limit of the slopes of secant lines; the instantaneous rate of change.
Riemann integral
The limit of sums of function values times lengths of small intervals.
Monotone function
A function that never decreases, or never increases, as its input increases.
Bounded variation
A function whose graph has finite total rise and fall; a difference of two monotone functions.
Absolutely continuous function
A function that is the integral of its derivative; stronger than continuity.
Convex function
A function whose graph lies on or below each chord joining two of its points.
