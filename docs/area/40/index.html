40 Sequences, series, summability

Sequences and series

What does it mean to add infinitely many numbers, and can a divergent sum still be given a sensible value?

This area studies infinite sequences and series of numbers and functions: when they converge, how fast, and what can be done when they do not. Besides convergence tests it covers infinite products, continued fractions seen as limits, and multiple series. A large part is summability theory, which assigns generalized sums to divergent series through averaging procedures such as those of Cesàro and Abel.

Summability methods are usually given by infinite matrices or integral means, and the main questions are which methods are consistent with ordinary convergence and how they compare. Tauberian theorems run the other way, recovering convergence from summability under a side condition. These ideas began with Fourier series and with power series on the boundary of convergence, and they are used in number theory, approximation theory and functional analysis.

Central objects

Convergent series
A series whose partial sums approach a finite limit.
Absolute convergence
Convergence of the series of absolute values; it allows free rearrangement of terms.
Conditional convergence
Convergence that is not absolute, so the sum depends on the order of terms.
Cesàro mean
The average of the first n partial sums of a series.
Abel summation
Assigning to ∑an\sum a_n∑an​ the limit of ∑anxn\sum a_nx^n∑an​xn as xxx increases to 1.
Regular summability method
A method that gives every convergent series its ordinary sum.
Tauberian condition
A side condition on the terms under which summability implies convergence.
