Algebraic topology

How can algebra prove that a doughnut surface can never be deformed into a sphere?

Algebraic topology attaches algebraic objects, such as numbers, groups and rings, to topological spaces in such a way that continuous deformations do not change them. If two spaces have different invariants, they cannot be deformed into each other. The main invariants are homotopy groups, which record maps of spheres into a space, and homology and cohomology groups, which detect holes of each dimension.

The invariants are functors: maps between spaces induce homomorphisms between groups, so topological problems become algebraic ones. Computation relies on exact sequences, spectral sequences, fibrations and cell decompositions. Homotopy theory studies spaces up to deformation and has grown into an abstract framework used across mathematics. The area gave rise to homological algebra and category theory, and it interacts with the geometry of manifolds, algebraic geometry and mathematical physics.

Counting turns around a hole

For a closed loop in the plane that avoids the origin, count how many times it winds around the origin, with counterclockwise turns positive. The loop t↦(cos⁡2t,sin⁡2t)t\mapsto(\cos2t,\sin2t)t↦(cos2t,sin2t), 0≤t≤2π0\le t\le2\pi0≤t≤2π, has winding number 2. Deforming a loop without crossing the origin never changes this integer, and two loops with the same number can be deformed into each other. So loops in the punctured plane are classified by integers: its fundamental group is Z\mathbb{Z}Z.

Loops that can shrink and loops that cannot

A loop is drawn on a sphere or a torus. Slide it and watch its length. The readout says whether it can be pulled to a point without leaving the surface.

What to notice
  • On the sphere, a circle of latitude slides to the pole and its length goes to zero. Every loop on a sphere can be shrunk this way.
  • On the torus, the loop around the tube keeps a fixed length as it slides, and the loop around the hole never gets shorter than the inner circle. Neither can reach a point.
  • A small loop on the torus can still shrink. What cannot is a loop that goes around the tube or around the hole.

Central objects

Homotopy
A continuous deformation of one map into another.
Fundamental group
The group of loops at a base point, taken up to homotopy, under concatenation.
Homology group
An abelian group measuring the holes of a given dimension in a space.
Cohomology ring
The dual counterpart of homology, which carries a product structure.
CW complex
A space built by attaching cells of increasing dimension along their boundaries.
Fibration
A map with the homotopy lifting property; over a path-connected base all fibers are homotopy equivalent.
Spectral sequence
A computational device that approximates a homology group through successive pages of simpler groups.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Homotopy groups of spheres

    The groups of homotopy classes of maps between spheres are known only in a limited range; finding their general pattern is a long-running program.

  • Whitehead's asphericity question

    Is every connected subcomplex of an aspherical two-dimensional CW complex itself aspherical?
