Integral equations

What if the unknown in an equation is a function hidden inside an integral?

In an integral equation the unknown function appears under an integral sign. A Fredholm equation such as u(x)−λ∫abK(x,t)u(t) dt=f(x)u(x)-\lambda\int_a^bK(x,t)u(t)\,dt=f(x)u(x)−λ∫ab​K(x,t)u(t)dt=f(x) integrates over a fixed interval. A Volterra equation integrates only up to the current point xxx, which suits processes with memory. The area studies existence and uniqueness of solutions, their dependence on the parameter λ\lambdaλ, and eigenvalue problems for the integral operator.

Many boundary value problems for differential equations can be recast as integral equations by means of Green functions or boundary layer potentials, often with better analytic and numerical behavior. Fredholm's study of these equations around 1900 led to Hilbert spaces, compact operators and spectral theory, so the subject is a parent of functional analysis. Singular kernels, as in the Cauchy and Wiener-Hopf equations, need methods from complex analysis.

Central objects

Kernel
The function K(x,t)K(x,t)K(x,t) that weights the unknown inside the integral.
Fredholm equation
An integral equation over a fixed domain; second kind means the unknown also appears outside the integral.
Volterra equation
An integral equation whose upper limit of integration is the variable itself.
Neumann series
The series solution obtained by iterating the integral operator, analogous to a geometric series.
Resolvent kernel
The kernel of the integral operator that expresses the solution in terms of the right-hand side.
Compact integral operator
An integral operator mapping bounded sets to sets with compact closure, as continuous kernels do.
Singular integral equation
An equation whose kernel is not integrable, so the integral is taken as a principal value.
