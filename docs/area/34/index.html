34 Ordinary differential equations

Ordinary diff. equations

If you know the rule for how a quantity changes at each instant, can you predict its whole future?

An ordinary differential equation relates an unknown function of one variable, usually time, to its derivatives. Newton's laws, population models and electrical circuits all take this form. The area asks when solutions exist and are unique, how they depend on initial data and parameters, how they behave as time goes to infinity, and what happens when conditions are imposed at the two ends of an interval.

Few equations can be solved in closed form, so the emphasis is on qualitative and asymptotic methods: phase portraits, stability analysis by linearization and Lyapunov functions, bifurcation theory, perturbation expansions and spectral theory of boundary value problems. Linear equations in the complex domain bring in monodromy and special functions. The field merges into dynamical systems, control theory and numerical analysis.

Central objects

Initial value problem
A differential equation together with the value of the solution at one starting time.
Phase portrait
The picture of all solution curves of a system in the space of states.
Equilibrium
A state at which the vector field vanishes, so the constant function is a solution.
Lyapunov function
A function that does not increase along solutions, used to prove stability without solving the equation.
Limit cycle
A periodic orbit that is isolated among periodic orbits.
Boundary value problem
A differential equation with conditions imposed at both ends of an interval.
Fundamental matrix
A matrix whose columns form a basis of the solutions of a linear system.
