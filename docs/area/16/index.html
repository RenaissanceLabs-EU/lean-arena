16 Associative rings and algebras

Associative rings

What changes in algebra when ababab and bababa are allowed to differ, as they do for matrices?

This area studies rings in which multiplication is associative but need not be commutative. The basic examples are rings of square matrices, the quaternions, group algebras, and rings of differential operators. A ring is understood largely through its modules, the objects on which it acts, in the way a group is understood through its representations.

Structure theory splits a ring into a radical and a semisimple part and classifies the simple pieces. Homological methods and Morita theory compare rings through their categories of modules. The area includes representations of finite-dimensional algebras by quivers, division algebras and the Brauer group, Hopf algebras and quantum groups, and it connects to group theory, algebraic geometry, operator algebras and mathematical physics.

Central objects

Ring
A set with addition and an associative multiplication that distributes over addition.
Module
An abelian group on which a ring acts linearly, generalizing a vector space.
Left, right and two-sided ideals
Additive subgroups closed under multiplication by ring elements on one side or on both.
Division ring
A ring in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.
Jacobson radical
The set of elements that act as zero on every simple module.
Semisimple ring
A ring all of whose modules are direct sums of simple modules.
Morita equivalence
Two rings are Morita equivalent when their categories of modules are equivalent.

Landmark theorems

  1. Frobenius's theorem on real division algebras1877

    Every finite-dimensional associative division algebra over the real numbers is isomorphic to R\mathbb{R}R, C\mathbb{C}C or the quaternions H\mathbb{H}H.

  2. Maschke's theorem

    If GGG is a finite group and kkk is a field whose characteristic does not divide the order of GGG, then the group algebra k[G]k[G]k[G] is semisimple.

  3. Wedderburn's little theorem1905

    Every finite division ring is commutative, hence a field.

  4. Wedderburn-Artin theorem1927

    A ring RRR is semisimple, meaning that every left RRR-module is a direct sum of simple modules, if and only if RRR is isomorphic to a finite product of matrix rings Mni(Di)M_{n_i}(D_i)Mni​​(Di​) over division rings DiD_iDi​. The numbers nin_ini​ and the rings DiD_iDi​ are determined up to order and isomorphism.

  5. Skolem-Noether theorem

    Let AAA be a finite-dimensional central simple algebra over a field kkk. Every kkk-algebra automorphism of AAA has the form x↦uxu−1x\mapsto uxu^{-1}x↦uxu−1 for some invertible uuu in AAA.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Köthe conjecture

    If a ring has no nonzero two-sided ideal consisting of nilpotent elements, then it has no nonzero one-sided ideal of that kind.

  • Kaplansky's zero divisor conjecture

    The group algebra of a torsion-free group over a field has no zero divisors.

  • Dixmier conjecture

    Every algebra endomorphism of a Weyl algebra over a field of characteristic 0 is an automorphism.
