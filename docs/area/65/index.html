Numerical analysis

How do you solve an equation on a machine that keeps only about sixteen digits of each number?

Numerical analysis designs and studies algorithms that compute approximate answers to mathematical problems: solving equations, evaluating integrals, and following the solutions of differential equations. Most problems from science and engineering have no closed-form solution, so they are replaced by finite computations. The area asks how accurate the result is, how much work it takes and how errors grow along the way.

Typical arguments bound the error of a method in terms of a step size or the number of iterations, and check that rounding errors are not amplified. Linear algebra is at the center, since most methods end in large systems of linear equations or eigenvalue problems. The area draws on approximation theory and functional analysis, and it supplies the algorithms used in scientific computing.

What to notice
  • With few iterations most of the plane is still unsettled. As the count rises, the three basins fill in around the roots.
  • The border between basins is never a clean line: near it, tiny changes in the start send the iteration to different roots.
  • Points close to a root settle in a step or two. Points near where the derivative is zero get thrown far away first.

Central objects

Floating-point arithmetic
Computer arithmetic with a fixed number of significant digits, so each operation is rounded.
Condition number
A measure of how much the answer to a problem changes when its data change slightly.
Numerical stability
The property that an algorithm does not amplify rounding and approximation errors unduly.
Discretization
Replacing a continuous problem by a finite one, for example on a grid or a mesh.
Order of convergence
The rate at which the error shrinks as the step size decreases or iterations proceed.
Iterative method
A procedure that improves an approximate solution step by step.
Quadrature rule
A formula that approximates an integral by a weighted sum of function values.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Growth factor for complete pivoting

    How large can the entries become during Gaussian elimination with complete pivoting on an n×nn \times nn×n real matrix? The true growth rate is unknown.

  • High-dimensional problems

    Finding methods for differential equations in many variables whose cost does not grow exponentially with the dimension.
