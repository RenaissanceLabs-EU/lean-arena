42 Harmonic analysis on Euclidean spaces

Harmonic analysis

Can every signal be split into pure tones, and does adding the tones back always return the signal?

Harmonic analysis on Euclidean spaces studies how functions on the circle, the line or Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn decompose into waves of pure frequency. Fourier series and the Fourier transform are the basic tools. The area asks in what sense a function equals the sum of its frequency components, how properties of a function such as smoothness and decay are reflected in its transform, and how operators act on these decompositions.

Much of the modern subject concerns bounds for operators: maximal functions, singular integrals such as the Hilbert transform, multipliers and oscillatory integrals. Standard techniques include interpolation, covering lemmas, decompositions into pieces localized in space and frequency, and wavelet or other time-frequency expansions. The results are basic working tools in partial differential equations, and they connect with number theory, geometric measure theory and signal processing.

Central objects

Fourier series
The expansion of a periodic function in sines and cosines, or complex exponentials, of integer frequencies.
Fourier transform
The map sending fff to f^(ξ)=∫f(x)e−2πix⋅ξ dx\hat f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,dxf^​(ξ)=∫f(x)e−2πix⋅ξdx, its amplitude at each frequency.
Convolution
The product (f∗g)(x)=∫f(x−y)g(y) dy(f*g)(x)=\int f(x-y)g(y)\,dy(f∗g)(x)=∫f(x−y)g(y)dy, which the Fourier transform turns into multiplication.
Maximal function
The supremum of the averages of a function over all balls centered at a point.
Singular integral
An operator given by convolution with a kernel too singular to be integrable, such as the Hilbert transform.
Fourier multiplier
An operator that multiplies the Fourier transform of a function by a fixed function.
Wavelet
A function whose translates and dilates form a basis localized in both position and scale.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Kakeya conjecture

    Must a compact set in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn that contains a unit segment in every direction have Hausdorff dimension nnn? This holds in the plane, a proof for n=3n=3n=3 was announced in 2025, and higher dimensions are open.

  • Fourier restriction conjecture

    For which exponents does the Fourier transform of an LpL^pLp function on Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn restrict to the unit sphere as an LqL^qLq function? The conjectured range is proved in the plane and open for n≥3n\ge3n≥3.
