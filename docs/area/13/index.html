Commutative algebra

Whole numbers factor into primes; what is the analog for polynomials in several variables?

Commutative algebra studies commutative rings, their ideals, and the modules over them. The model examples are the integers and the rings of polynomials in several variables over a field, together with rings built from these by taking quotients, fractions and completions. Ideals generalize the notion of divisibility, and prime ideals play the role that prime numbers play among the integers.

It is the local algebra behind algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory. A system of polynomial equations corresponds to an ideal, and the geometry of its solution set is read off from the quotient ring. Typical techniques are localization at a prime, completion, dimension theory, and homological methods such as free resolutions and depth. Gröbner bases make many of the constructions computable.

A line with a thickened point

In the polynomial ring k[x,y]k[x,y]k[x,y] over a field, take the ideal I=(x2,xy)I=(x^2,xy)I=(x2,xy). Its zero set in the plane is the line x=0x=0x=0. Central objects

Commutative ring
A set with addition and multiplication obeying the usual rules, including ab=baab=baab=ba.
Ideal
A subset closed under addition and under multiplication by every element of the ring.
Prime ideal
A proper ideal PPP such that ab∈Pab\in Pab∈P implies a∈Pa\in Pa∈P or b∈Pb\in Pb∈P.
Module
The analog of a vector space with scalars taken from a ring instead of a field.
Localization
A ring of fractions formed by allowing chosen elements as denominators.
Noetherian ring
A ring in which every ideal is generated by finitely many elements.
Krull dimension
The largest length of a chain of prime ideals, matching the geometric dimension.

Landmark theorems

  1. Hilbert's basis theorem1890

    If RRR is a Noetherian commutative ring, then so is the polynomial ring R[x]R[x]R[x]. In particular, every ideal of k[x1,…,xn]k[x_1,\dots,x_n]k[x1​,…,xn​] with kkk a field is finitely generated.

  2. Hilbert's Nullstellensatz1893

    Let kkk be an algebraically closed field and III an ideal of k[x1,…,xn]k[x_1,\dots,x_n]k[x1​,…,xn​]. If a polynomial fff vanishes at every common zero of III in knk^nkn, then some power of fff lies in III.

  3. Lasker-Noether theorem1921

    In a Noetherian commutative ring, every ideal is an intersection of finitely many primary ideals.

  4. Krull's principal ideal theorem1928

    In a Noetherian commutative ring, every prime ideal that is minimal among the primes containing a given principal ideal has height at most 1.

  5. Nakayama's lemma

    Let MMM be a finitely generated module over a commutative ring RRR, and let III be an ideal contained in the Jacobson radical of RRR. If IM=MIM=MIM=M, then M=0M=0M=0.

  6. Quillen-Suslin theorem1976

    Every finitely generated projective module over a polynomial ring k[x1,…,xn]k[x_1,\dots,x_n]k[x1​,…,xn​] over a field kkk is free.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Serre's positivity conjecture

    Whether Serre's intersection multiplicity of two modules over a regular local ring is positive when their dimensions add up to the dimension of the ring; open in mixed characteristic.

  • Small Cohen-Macaulay modules

    Whether every complete Noetherian local domain has a finitely generated module whose depth equals the dimension of the ring; open from dimension 3 on.
