80 Classical thermodynamics, heat transfer

Thermodynamics and heat

Why does heat flow from hot to cold and never the other way on its own?

Classical thermodynamics describes matter in bulk through a few quantities such as temperature, pressure, volume, energy and entropy, without reference to atoms. Its laws limit what any engine, refrigerator or chemical process can do. Heat transfer studies how thermal energy moves by conduction, convection and radiation, and includes melting and freezing, combustion and chemically reacting systems.

Mathematically, thermodynamics is a theory of state functions and inequalities, with convexity and Legendre transforms relating the different potentials. Heat conduction leads to the heat equation, the model parabolic partial differential equation, and phase change leads to free boundary problems in which the interface is part of the unknown. Statistical mechanics explains the laws from microscopic models, and continuum mechanics uses them as constraints.

The best possible engine

An engine takes heat from a hot source at Th=500T_h = 500Th​=500 K and rejects heat to surroundings at Tc=300T_c = 300Tc​=300 K. Carnot's theorem limits its efficiency to 1−Tc/Th=1−300/500=0.41 - T_c/T_h = 1 - 300/500 = 0.41−Tc​/Th​=1−300/500=0.4. So from every 1000 J of heat taken in, at most 400 J can become work, and at least 600 J must be discarded. No design can do better between these two temperatures.

Central objects

Temperature
The quantity that is equal for two bodies in thermal equilibrium with each other.
Internal energy
The energy stored in a system, changed by heat flow and by work.
Entropy
A state function that never decreases in an isolated system and measures irreversibility.
Equation of state
A relation among pressure, volume and temperature, such as the ideal gas law pV=nRTpV = nRTpV=nRT.
Free energy
A thermodynamic potential that is minimized at equilibrium under fixed temperature.
Heat flux
The rate of flow of thermal energy per unit area.
Free boundary
A moving interface, such as a melting front, whose position must be found along with the temperature.

Landmark theorems

  1. First law of thermodynamics

    The change in internal energy of a closed system equals the heat supplied to it minus the work it does: dU=δQ−δWdU = \delta Q - \delta WdU=δQ−δW.

  2. Second law of thermodynamics

    No process has as its sole result the transfer of heat from a colder body to a hotter one. Equivalently, for any cyclic process ∮δQ/T≤0\oint \delta Q/T \le 0∮δQ/T≤0, with equality for reversible cycles, and the entropy of an isolated system never decreases.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Thermodynamics far from equilibrium

    Find general principles, comparable to the classical laws, that govern systems driven strongly away from equilibrium.
