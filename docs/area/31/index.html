31 Potential theory

Potential theory

What temperature settles inside a metal plate when the temperature along its edge is held fixed?

Potential theory began as the study of gravitational and electrostatic potentials, which in empty space satisfy Laplace's equation. Its central objects are harmonic functions, their relatives the subharmonic and superharmonic functions, and the potentials generated by distributions of mass or charge. It asks how such functions behave, how they are determined by boundary values, and how to measure the size of small sets by capacity.

Methods include the mean value property, maximum principles, Green functions, and the Perron method of taking upper envelopes of subharmonic functions. There is a close dictionary with probability: harmonic functions are averages over Brownian paths, and capacity measures how likely a path is to hit a set. The area connects complex analysis, elliptic partial differential equations, several complex variables through plurisubharmonic functions, and Markov processes.

Central objects

Harmonic function
A twice differentiable function uuu with Δu=0\Delta u=0Δu=0.
Subharmonic function
An upper semicontinuous function whose value at each point is at most its average over small surrounding spheres.
Newtonian potential
The convolution of a mass distribution with the fundamental solution of the Laplacian.
Green function
The potential of a unit point charge in a domain whose boundary is held at potential zero.
Capacity
A measure of the size of a set by the charge it can hold at bounded potential.
Harmonic measure
The distribution of the point where Brownian motion started inside a domain first hits the boundary.
Polar set
A set so small that Brownian motion almost surely never hits it at positive times.

Landmark theorems

  1. Mean value property

    If uuu is harmonic on an open set containing a closed ball, then the value of uuu at the center equals the average of uuu over the bounding sphere. Conversely, a continuous function with this property for all such balls is harmonic.

  2. Maximum principle

    A harmonic function on a connected open set that attains its maximum at some point of the set is constant.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Dimension of harmonic measure in space

    In the plane, harmonic measure always sits on a set of Hausdorff dimension at most 1. For domains in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3 the largest possible dimension is unknown; it is known to lie strictly between 2 and 3.
