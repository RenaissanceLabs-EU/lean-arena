78 Optics, electromagnetic theory

Optics, electromagnetism

Why does a straw look bent in a glass of water, and what is light made of?

This area covers the mathematics of electric and magnetic fields and of light. Charges and currents create fields, changing fields create each other, and the resulting waves include radio, visible light and X-rays. Topics include electrostatics and magnetostatics, wave propagation, antennas and waveguides, scattering and diffraction, geometric optics with its rays and lenses, and the behavior of fields in materials.

Everything rests on Maxwell's equations, a system of linear partial differential equations for the fields, together with laws describing the material. Methods include potential theory, Fourier analysis, integral equations, asymptotic approximations for short wavelengths and numerical simulation. Inverse problems, where one infers an object from the waves it scatters, form a large modern part. The theory led to special relativity and to quantum electrodynamics.

Central objects

Electric and magnetic fields
Vector fields EEE and BBB that determine the force on a moving charge.
Electromagnetic wave
A self-sustaining oscillation of electric and magnetic fields that travels through space.
Refractive index
The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in a material.
Polarization
The direction in which the electric field of a wave oscillates.
Diffraction
The spreading and interference of waves that pass an obstacle or an opening.
Scalar and vector potentials
Auxiliary fields from which the electric and magnetic fields are obtained by differentiation.
Waveguide
A structure, such as a metal tube or an optical fiber, that guides waves along its length.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Radiation reaction

    Find a fully consistent classical equation of motion for a point charge that includes the force from its own radiation; the standard candidate has unphysical runaway solutions.

  • Inverse scattering with limited data

    Under what conditions do measurements of scattered waves from a few directions or frequencies determine the scattering object uniquely?
