22 Topological groups, Lie groups

Lie groups

How do you do algebra and calculus at once on symmetries that vary smoothly, like rotations of a sphere?

A topological group is a group that is also a space, with continuous multiplication and inversion. A Lie group is one that is a smooth manifold with smooth operations, such as the rotations of space or the invertible n×nn\times nn×n real matrices. These groups describe continuous symmetry: symmetries that can be varied gradually, unlike the finitely many symmetries of a square.

A Lie group is studied through its Lie algebra, the tangent space at the identity, which turns many questions into linear algebra. Integration against Haar measure allows averaging over a group. Representation theory decomposes functions on the group, or on spaces on which it acts, in a way that generalizes Fourier series. The area connects to differential geometry, harmonic analysis, number theory through automorphic forms, and quantum physics.

Central objects

Topological group
A group with a topology for which multiplication and inversion are continuous.
Lie group
A group that is a smooth manifold, with smooth multiplication and inversion.
Lie algebra of a group
The tangent space at the identity with the bracket induced by the group law.
Exponential map
The map from the Lie algebra to the group that follows one-parameter subgroups for unit time.
Haar measure
A measure on a locally compact group that is unchanged by left translation.
Unitary representation
A continuous action of the group on a Hilbert space by unitary operators.
Homogeneous space
A space on which the group acts transitively, such as a sphere under rotations.

Landmark theorems

  1. Lie's third theorem

    Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of a simply connected Lie group, and that group is unique up to isomorphism. The global form stated here is due to Cartan.

  2. Peter-Weyl theorem1927

    Let GGG be a compact Hausdorff group. The matrix coefficients of its finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations span a dense subspace of the continuous functions on GGG with the uniform norm, and every irreducible unitary representation of GGG is finite-dimensional.

  3. Closed subgroup theorem

    Every closed subgroup of a Lie group is an embedded submanifold, and hence is itself a Lie group.

  4. Existence of Haar measure1933

    Every locally compact Hausdorff group carries a nonzero Radon measure invariant under left translation, and it is unique up to a positive constant factor. Haar proved existence in 1933 for second countable groups; the general case and uniqueness followed within a few years.

  5. Pontryagin duality

    Let GGG be a locally compact abelian Hausdorff group and G^\hat GG^ the group of continuous homomorphisms from GGG to the circle group, with the compact-open topology. Then the evaluation map from GGG to the dual of G^\hat GG^ is an isomorphism of topological groups.

  6. Solution of Hilbert's fifth problem1952

    If a topological group is locally Euclidean, meaning that it is a topological manifold, then it has a smooth structure making it a Lie group. This follows from work of Gleason and of Montgomery and Zippin.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Hilbert-Smith conjecture

    A locally compact group that acts faithfully on a connected manifold is a Lie group; known for manifolds of dimension at most 3.

  • Unitary dual problem

    Describe all irreducible unitary representations of a real reductive Lie group; an algorithm is known, but an explicit description is not.
