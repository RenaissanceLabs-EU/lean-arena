92 Biology and other natural sciences

Mathematical biology

How fast will an epidemic spread, and what share of people must be immune to stop it?

This area gathers the mathematics used in the life sciences and chemistry. It includes models of how populations grow and compete, how diseases spread, how genes change in frequency over generations, how nerve cells fire, how tissues take shape, and how molecules react. It also covers methods for biological data, such as comparing DNA sequences and reconstructing evolutionary trees.

Most models are dynamical systems. Ordinary differential equations describe well-mixed populations and chemical concentrations, partial differential equations add space, and stochastic processes handle small numbers and chance events. Graphs and combinatorics enter through networks and sequences. A model is judged by whether it explains data and predicts the result of an intervention, so parameter estimation and statistics are part of the work.

Two chemicals that make animal patterns

The Gray-Scott model: chemical U is fed in, chemical V eats U and decays, and both spread out, V more slowly. Pick a pattern, press Reset, or drag on the picture to add V.

What to notice
  • Nothing in the rules mentions spots or stripes. They appear because V spreads more slowly than U, which Alan Turing showed can break a uniform state.
  • Only the feed rate F and kill rate k differ between the patterns. Small changes give spots, worms, splitting blobs or coral.
  • Dragging adds a patch of V. Where it lands, the pattern grows around it.

Central objects

Population model
An equation describing how the size of one or more populations changes in time.
Compartmental model
A model that divides a population into groups, such as susceptible, infected and recovered.
Basic reproduction number
The average number of new infections caused by one case in a fully susceptible population.
Reaction network
A set of chemical species and the reactions that convert them into one another.
Reaction-diffusion system
Equations for substances that react locally and spread through space.
Allele frequency
The proportion of copies of a gene in a population that are of a given variant.
Phylogenetic tree
A branching diagram representing the evolutionary relationships among species or genes.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Global attractor conjecture

    Does every complex-balanced mass-action reaction system converge to its positive equilibrium from every positive starting point with the same conserved quantities? A proof has been announced but is not yet generally accepted.

  • From genotype to phenotype

    Predict the traits of an organism from its genome and environment.

  • Multiscale models of living systems

    Link models of molecules, cells, tissues and whole organisms in a mathematically controlled way.
