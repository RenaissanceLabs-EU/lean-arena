74 Mechanics of deformable solids

Solid mechanics

How much load can a slender column carry before it suddenly bows sideways?

Solid mechanics describes how solid bodies deform, vibrate and break under loads. The body is modeled as a continuum, and the unknowns are the displacement of each point and the internal forces, called stresses. The area covers elastic materials that spring back, plastic materials that deform permanently, materials with memory, thin structures such as beams, plates and shells, and the growth of cracks.

The governing equations combine a balance law for momentum with a constitutive law that describes the material. They are systems of partial differential equations, linear for small elastic deformations and nonlinear otherwise. Equilibrium states are often found as minimizers of an energy, which links the area to the calculus of variations. Practical computation relies mostly on the finite element method.

Central objects

Displacement field
The vector by which each point of the body has moved from its reference position.
Strain tensor
A symmetric tensor measuring local stretching and shearing.
Stress tensor
A tensor giving the force per unit area across any small surface inside the body.
Constitutive law
The relation between stress and strain that characterizes a particular material.
Elastic modulus
A material constant, such as Young's modulus, relating stress to strain in the elastic range.
Stored energy function
The elastic energy per unit volume as a function of the deformation gradient.
Crack
A surface inside the body across which the displacement may jump.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Regularity of energy minimizers

    Are the energy minimizers of nonlinear elasticity smooth, and do they satisfy the equilibrium equations in the weak sense?

  • Dynamics in nonlinear elasticity

    Do the equations of motion of nonlinear elasticity in three dimensions have global weak solutions for general initial data?

  • Prediction of crack paths

    Finding mathematically justified models that predict when cracks start, where they go and how they branch.
