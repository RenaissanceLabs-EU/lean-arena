Integral transforms

How can a transform turn a differential equation into a problem of plain algebra?

An integral transform turns a function fff into a new function by integrating it against a fixed kernel: (Tf)(s)=∫K(s,t)f(t) dt(Tf)(s)=\int K(s,t)f(t)\,dt(Tf)(s)=∫K(s,t)f(t)dt. The Laplace, Fourier, Mellin, Hankel, Hilbert and Radon transforms are the best known. The area studies which functions each transform accepts, how to invert it, and how it converts operations such as differentiation and convolution into simpler ones.

The guiding idea is to move a hard problem to a setting where it becomes easy, solve it there, and transform back. Differential equations become algebraic equations, and convolutions become products. Operational calculus makes this systematic by treating differentiation as an algebraic symbol. The subject also includes moment problems, which ask when a measure is determined by its moments. It supports differential equations, probability, signal processing and tomography.
