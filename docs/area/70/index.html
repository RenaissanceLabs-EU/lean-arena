70 Mechanics of particles and systems

Classical mechanics

Given where the planets are tonight, where will they be a thousand years from now?

This area is classical mechanics: the motion of point masses, rigid bodies and systems built from them, under forces such as gravity, springs and constraints. It includes the motion of planets and satellites, spinning tops, pendulums, vibrating systems and machines with linked parts. Bodies that deform, such as elastic solids and fluids, are treated in neighboring areas.

Newton's laws turn a mechanical problem into ordinary differential equations. The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations rewrite these equations in terms of a single function, the energy or a close relative, and make symmetries and conserved quantities visible. Hamiltonian mechanics is the origin of symplectic geometry, and questions about long-term behavior of orbits led to the modern theory of dynamical systems.

Central objects

Configuration space
The set of all positions a system can take; its dimension is the number of degrees of freedom.
Phase space
The space of all positions together with momenta; a point determines the future motion.
Lagrangian
Kinetic minus potential energy, as a function of positions and velocities.
Hamiltonian
The total energy expressed as a function of positions and momenta.
Conserved quantity
A function of the state, such as energy or angular momentum, that stays constant along every motion.
Rigid body
An idealized body whose points keep fixed distances from each other.
Constraint
A restriction on the possible positions or velocities, such as a bead forced to stay on a wire.
