Operator theory

Can an infinite matrix be diagonalized, and what plays the role of its eigenvalues?

Operator theory studies linear maps, called operators, on Banach and Hilbert spaces, along with some nonlinear ones. Differentiation, integration against a kernel and multiplication by a function are typical examples. The central concept is the spectrum, which generalizes the set of eigenvalues of a matrix. The area classifies operators by their spectral behavior and studies special classes: compact, self-adjoint, normal, Fredholm, and unbounded operators such as differential operators.

The aim is a structure theory comparable to diagonalization and Jordan form for matrices. Tools include the spectral theorem, functional calculus, resolvents, semigroups of operators that describe time evolution, and index theory. Fixed point theorems and monotone operators handle nonlinear problems. Quantum mechanics is built on self-adjoint operators, and the subject works closely with functional analysis, differential equations, complex function theory and operator algebras.

Central objects

Bounded operator
A continuous linear map from a normed space to itself or to another normed space.
Spectrum
The set of scalars λ\lambdaλ for which T−λIT-\lambda IT−λI has no bounded inverse.
Resolvent
The operator-valued function λ↦(T−λI)−1\lambda\mapsto(T-\lambda I)^{-1}λ↦(T−λI)−1, defined off the spectrum.
Compact operator
An operator sending bounded sets to sets with compact closure; the closest relative of a matrix.
Self-adjoint operator
An operator on a Hilbert space equal to its own adjoint, the analog of a Hermitian matrix.
Fredholm index
The dimension of the kernel minus the codimension of the range, when both are finite.
Operator semigroup
A family T(t)T(t)T(t), t≥0t\ge0t≥0, with T(s+t)=T(s)T(t)T(s+t)=T(s)T(t)T(s+t)=T(s)T(t), describing evolution in time.

Landmark theorems

  1. Riesz theory of compact operators1918

    Let KKK be a compact operator on a Banach space and λ≠0\lambda\ne0λ=0. Then K−λIK-\lambda IK−λI is injective if and only if it is surjective. Every nonzero point of the spectrum of KKK is an eigenvalue of finite multiplicity, and the nonzero eigenvalues can accumulate only at 0.

  2. Banach fixed-point theorem1922

    Let fff be a map of a nonempty complete metric space to itself with d(f(x),f(y))≤q d(x,y)d(f(x),f(y))\le q\,d(x,y)d(f(x),f(y))≤qd(x,y) for some q<1q<1q<1. Then fff has exactly one fixed point, and the iterates fn(x)f^n(x)fn(x) converge to it from every starting point xxx.

  3. Spectral theorem

    Every bounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space is unitarily equivalent to the operator of multiplication by a bounded real measurable function on L2(μ)L^2(\mu)L2(μ) for some measure space. The same holds for unbounded self-adjoint operators, with a real measurable function that need not be bounded.

  4. Stone's theorem1932

    The strongly continuous one-parameter groups of unitary operators on a Hilbert space are exactly the groups U(t)=eitAU(t)=e^{itA}U(t)=eitA with AAA a self-adjoint operator, possibly unbounded. The operator AAA is determined by the group.

  5. Hille-Yosida theorem1948

    A linear operator AAA on a Banach space generates a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions if and only if AAA is closed and densely defined and, for every λ>0\lambda>0λ>0, the operator λI−A\lambda I-AλI−A has a bounded inverse of norm at most 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ.

  6. Atkinson's theorem1951

    A bounded operator on a Hilbert space is Fredholm if and only if it is invertible modulo the compact operators.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Invariant subspace problem

    Does every bounded operator on an infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space have a closed invariant subspace other than zero and the whole space? Counterexamples exist on some Banach spaces.
