19 \(K\)-theory

K-theory

A Möbius band is twisted, yet two of them combine into something untwisted. How do you keep the books?

K-theory attaches abelian groups to a ring, a space or an algebraic variety by formally adding and subtracting the vector bundles or projective modules that live on it. The group K0K_0K0​ records these objects up to a stable form of equivalence, the group K1K_1K1​ records invertible matrices up to elementary operations, and higher groups continue the pattern.

There are several versions. Topological K-theory is a cohomology theory for spaces, governed by Bott periodicity. Algebraic K-theory of rings and schemes is much harder to compute and carries arithmetic information, such as class groups and special values of zeta functions. Operator K-theory applies to C*-algebras. The subject grew out of the Riemann-Roch theorem and remains tied to index theory, the topology of manifolds and motivic cohomology.

Two Möbius bands

Over the circle there are two real line bundles: the trivial one, a cylinder, and the Möbius band MMM. They are not isomorphic. Yet the direct sum M⊕MM\oplus MM⊕M is isomorphic to the trivial plane bundle over the circle. In K-theory the class [M]−[1][M]-[1][M]−[1] is therefore not zero, but twice it is zero. In fact the reduced real K-group of the circle is Z/2\mathbb{Z}/2Z/2, generated by this class.

Central objects

Vector bundle
A family of vector spaces that varies continuously over the points of a space.
Projective module
A direct summand of a free module; the algebraic counterpart of a vector bundle.
Grothendieck group
The abelian group obtained from a commutative monoid by adjoining formal differences.
The group K0K_0K0​
The Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective modules, or of vector bundles, under direct sum.
The group K1K_1K1​
The group of invertible matrices of all sizes over a ring, made abelian.
Higher K-groups
Homotopy groups of a space built from the category of projective modules, as defined by Quillen.
Chern character
A ring homomorphism from K-theory to rational cohomology; for finite complexes an isomorphism after tensoring with the rationals.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Farrell-Jones conjecture

    Predicts that the algebraic K-theory of a group ring can be computed from the virtually cyclic subgroups of the group; proved for many classes of groups, open in general.

  • Baum-Connes conjecture

    Predicts that an assembly map computes the K-theory of the reduced C*-algebra of a group from the proper actions of the group; open in general.

  • K-groups of the integers

    Whether K4i(Z)=0K_{4i}(\mathbb{Z})=0K4i​(Z)=0 for every i≥1i\ge 1i≥1; this is equivalent to Vandiver's conjecture in number theory.
