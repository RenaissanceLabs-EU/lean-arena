17 Nonassociative rings and algebras

Nonassociative algebras

What is left of algebra when (ab)c(ab)c(ab)c need not equal a(bc)a(bc)a(bc)?

This area studies algebras whose multiplication is not assumed to be associative. The most studied are Lie algebras, in which the product is a bracket [x,y][x,y][x,y] that changes sign when its arguments are swapped and satisfies the Jacobi identity. Other classes are Jordan algebras, alternative algebras such as the octonions, and super and infinite-dimensional versions of all of these.

Lie algebras arise as the infinitesimal form of continuous symmetry: the tangent space at the identity of a Lie group is a Lie algebra, and much of the group can be recovered from it. Classification proceeds through root systems and Dynkin diagrams, and representation theory through weights and characters. Infinite-dimensional examples such as Kac-Moody and Virasoro algebras, and quantum groups, are standard tools in mathematical physics.

The cross product

For vectors in space, the cross product satisfies a×b=−b×aa\times b=-b\times aa×b=−b×a, and it is not associative. With the standard unit vectors, (i×i)×j=0(i\times i)\times j=0(i×i)×j=0 while i×(i×j)=i×k=−ji\times(i\times j)=i\times k=-ji×(i×j)=i×k=−j. In place of associativity it obeys the Jacobi identity a×(b×c)+b×(c×a)+c×(a×b)=0a\times(b\times c)+b\times(c\times a)+c\times(a\times b)=0a×(b×c)+b×(c×a)+c×(a×b)=0. This makes three-dimensional space a Lie algebra, the one that belongs to the group of rotations.

Central objects

Lie algebra
A vector space with a bilinear bracket satisfying [x,x]=0[x,x]=0[x,x]=0 and the Jacobi identity.
Jacobi identity
The rule [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0, which replaces associativity.
Root system
A finite configuration of vectors, closed under certain reflections, that encodes a semisimple Lie algebra.
Universal enveloping algebra
An associative algebra whose modules are the same as the representations of a given Lie algebra.
Jordan algebra
A commutative algebra satisfying (xy)(xx)=x(y(xx))(xy)(xx)=x(y(xx))(xy)(xx)=x(y(xx)), modeled on symmetric matrices.
Octonions
An eight-dimensional real division algebra that is neither commutative nor associative.
Kac-Moody algebra
A Lie algebra, usually infinite-dimensional, defined by generators and relations from a generalized Cartan matrix.

  1. Classification of simple Lie algebras1894

    Every finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over C\mathbb{C}C is isomorphic to exactly one of the classical algebras An (n≥1)A_n\ (n\ge1)An​ (n≥1), Bn (n≥2)B_n\ (n\ge2)Bn​ (n≥2), Cn (n≥3)C_n\ (n\ge3)Cn​ (n≥3), Dn (n≥4)D_n\ (n\ge4)Dn​ (n≥4) or one of the five exceptional algebras E6,E7,E8,F4,G2E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4,G_2E6​,E7​,E8​,F4​,G2​.   2. Hurwitz's theorem1898

    Let AAA be a finite-dimensional real algebra with identity, not assumed associative, with a positive definite quadratic form NNN such that N(xy)=N(x)N(y)N(xy)=N(x)N(y)N(xy)=N(x)N(y). Then AAA is isomorphic to the real numbers, the complex numbers, the quaternions or the octonions, so its dimension is 1, 2, 4 or 8.

  3. Levi decomposition1905

    Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic 0 is the semidirect product of its largest solvable ideal and a semisimple subalgebra.

  4. Weyl's theorem on complete reducibility

    Every finite-dimensional representation of a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra over a field of characteristic 0 is a direct sum of irreducible representations.

  5. Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem

    Let g\mathfrak gg be a Lie algebra over a field with a totally ordered basis (xi)(x_i)(xi​).   6. Ado's theorem1935

    Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic 0 is isomorphic to a Lie algebra of square matrices with the bracket [X,Y]=XY−YX[X,Y]=XY-YX[X,Y]=XY−YX.
