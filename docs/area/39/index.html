39 Difference and functional equations

Difference equations

Which functions satisfy f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y), and what does a rule linking each term to earlier ones force?

A difference equation relates the values of a sequence, or of a function, at shifted arguments, as in the Fibonacci rule an+2=an+1+ana_{n+2}=a_{n+1}+a_nan+2​=an+1​+an​. It is the discrete counterpart of a differential equation. A functional equation asks for all functions satisfying a given identity, such as f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y). The area studies the solutions of both kinds of equation, their growth, their stability and their oscillation.

Linear recurrences are handled with characteristic polynomials, generating functions and discrete analogs of calculus, with summation in place of integration. Functional equations need regularity arguments: a weak assumption such as measurability often forces the solutions to have a simple explicit form. The area borders dynamical systems, since iterating a map is a difference equation, and it touches combinatorics, special functions, numerical analysis and economics.

Central objects

Recurrence relation
A rule expressing each term of a sequence through earlier terms.
Characteristic polynomial
The polynomial whose roots give the exponential solutions of a linear recurrence with constant coefficients.
Difference operator
The operator Δf(x)=f(x+1)−f(x)\Delta f(x)=f(x+1)-f(x)Δf(x)=f(x+1)−f(x), a discrete analog of the derivative.

The equation f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y), whose solutions are the additive functions.
Hyers-Ulam stability
The question whether every approximate solution of a functional equation lies near an exact one.

Open problems and frontiers
