35 Partial differential equations

Partial diff. equations

How do heat, sound and water waves evolve, and can the equations that govern them ever break down?

A partial differential equation relates an unknown function of several variables to its partial derivatives. The Laplace, heat and wave equations are the classical models, standing for equilibrium, diffusion and vibration. They also represent the three main types: elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic. The area asks whether solutions exist, whether they are unique, how smooth they are, and how they behave over long times or near singularities.

Modern work treats solutions as elements of function spaces such as Sobolev spaces and proves a priori estimates: bounds that any solution must satisfy. Tools include energy methods, maximum principles, Fourier analysis, variational methods and weak or distributional solutions. Nonlinear equations from fluid mechanics, geometry and relativity drive much of the research, and the field leans on functional analysis, harmonic analysis and differential geometry.

What to notice
  • Under the wave equation a poke becomes a ring that spreads at a fixed speed, bounces off the held edges and flips sign. The slight damping slowly lets the motion die out.
  • Under the heat equation a poke only spreads out and flattens. Nothing travels, nothing reflects, and the sheet relaxes toward zero.
  • Both schemes are explicit, and each time step respects its stability limit. The readout shows how close to the limit it runs.

Central objects

Elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic
The three main types of equation, modeled on the Laplace, heat and wave equations.
Weak solution
A function that satisfies the equation after integration against smooth test functions.
Sobolev space
A space of functions whose weak derivatives up to a given order have finite integral norms.
Fundamental solution
A solution with a point source, from which other solutions are built by convolution.
Well-posed problem
A problem whose solution exists, is unique, and depends continuously on the data.
Characteristics
Curves or surfaces along which a hyperbolic equation carries signals and singularities.
A priori estimate
A bound that every solution must satisfy, proved before solutions are known to exist.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness

    Do smooth, rapidly decaying initial data for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations always give a solution that stays smooth for all time?

  • Hot spots conjecture

    For a convex planar domain, does the second Neumann eigenfunction of the Laplacian attain its maximum and minimum only on the boundary?
