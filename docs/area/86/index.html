86 Geophysics

Geophysics

How do we know what the inside of the Earth looks like when nobody has ever been there?

Geophysics applies mathematics and physics to the Earth: its solid interior, oceans, atmosphere and magnetic field. Topics include earthquakes and the waves they send through the planet, the shape and gravity of the Earth, slow convection in the mantle that drives the plates, the generation of the magnetic field in the liquid core, and the circulation of air and water that makes weather and climate.

Almost nothing below the surface can be observed directly, so the area depends on inverse problems: inferring internal structure from measurements made at the surface or from satellites. The forward models come from elasticity and fluid dynamics on a rotating sphere. Data are noisy and incomplete, and the governing equations are often chaotic, so statistics, numerical simulation and data assimilation are central.

How far away was the earthquake?

An earthquake sends out fast P waves and slower S waves. Suppose they travel at 6 km/s and 3.5 km/s, and a station records the S wave 30 seconds after the P wave. The distance ddd satisfies d/3.5−d/6=30d/3.5 - d/6 = 30d/3.5−d/6=30, so d=252d = 252d=252 km. One station gives only a circle of possible locations. Circles from three stations meet in a single point, the epicenter.

Central objects

Seismic wave
An elastic wave traveling through the Earth, generated by an earthquake or an explosion.
Geoid
The surface of constant gravity potential that coincides with mean sea level.
Coriolis force
The apparent force that deflects moving air and water on the rotating Earth.
Mantle convection
The slow creeping flow of the solid mantle that carries heat outward and moves the plates.
Geodynamo
The process by which flow of liquid iron in the outer core maintains the magnetic field.
Inverse problem
Determining causes or internal structure from indirect measurements of their effects.
Data assimilation
Combining a forecast model with new observations to estimate the current state of a system.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Earthquake prediction

    Can the time, place and size of individual large earthquakes be forecast with useful accuracy?

  • Reversals of the magnetic field

    Explain and simulate, at realistic parameter values, why the geodynamo reverses polarity at irregular intervals.

  • Limits of climate prediction

    How precisely can the response of the climate system to a given forcing be determined?
