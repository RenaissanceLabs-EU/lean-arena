Fields and polynomials

Why is there a formula for the roots of a quadratic equation but none for the general equation of degree five?

Field theory studies number systems in which one can add, subtract, multiply and divide, such as the rational, real and complex numbers and the finite fields used in coding. It asks how a field can be enlarged by adjoining roots of polynomials, and what the symmetries of such an enlargement reveal about the polynomial.

Galois theory is the core method. It translates questions about the roots of a polynomial into questions about a finite group of permutations of those roots. This explains why equations of degree five have no general solution by radicals and which constructions are possible with ruler and compass. The area also covers ordered and real closed fields, valued fields, differential fields, and the location of roots of polynomials.

Central objects

Field
A set with addition and multiplication obeying the usual rules, in which nonzero elements have inverses.
Field extension
A field containing a given smaller field; its degree is its dimension over the smaller field.
Minimal polynomial
The monic polynomial of least degree over a field that has a given element as a root.
Splitting field
The smallest extension over which a given polynomial factors into linear factors.
Galois group
The group of automorphisms of an extension field that fix every element of the base field.
Algebraic closure
An algebraic extension in which every nonconstant polynomial has a root.
Finite field
A field with finitely many elements, such as the integers modulo a prime ppp.

Landmark theorems

  1. Fundamental theorem of algebra

    Every nonconstant polynomial with complex coefficients has a complex root.

  2. Fundamental theorem of Galois theory

    Let L/KL/KL/K be a finite Galois extension with group GGG. Sending a subgroup HHH to its fixed field LHL^HLH is an inclusion-reversing bijection between the subgroups of GGG and the fields between KKK and LLL. The field LHL^HLH is Galois over KKK exactly when HHH is normal in GGG.

  3. Abel-Ruffini theorem1824

    For each n≥5n\ge 5n≥5 there is no formula that expresses the roots of the general polynomial of degree nnn in terms of its coefficients using only field operations and extraction of roots. For example, the roots of x5−x−1x^5-x-1x5−x−1 cannot be expressed in this way starting from rational numbers.

  4. Galois's solvability criterion

    A polynomial over a field KKK of characteristic 0 is solvable by radicals if and only if the Galois group of its splitting field over KKK is a solvable group.

  5. Classification of finite fields

    The number of elements of a finite field is pnp^npn for some prime ppp and integer n≥1n\ge 1n≥1. For each such prime power there is, up to isomorphism, exactly one field with that many elements, and its nonzero elements form a cyclic group under multiplication.

  6. Artin's solution of Hilbert's seventeenth problem1927

    A polynomial in nnn variables with real coefficients that takes no negative value on Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn is a sum of squares of rational functions with real coefficients.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Inverse Galois problem

    Is every finite group the Galois group of some Galois extension of Q\mathbb{Q}Q?

  • Shafarevich's conjecture

    The absolute Galois group of the maximal abelian extension of Q\mathbb{Q}Q is a free profinite group of countable rank.
