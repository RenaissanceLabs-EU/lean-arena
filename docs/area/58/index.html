Global analysis

What can the frequencies at which a drum vibrates tell you about its shape?

Global analysis studies calculus and differential equations on curved spaces, called manifolds, instead of on flat Euclidean space. Typical questions ask how the solutions of an equation on a manifold, or the critical points of a function on it, reflect the overall shape of the space. The area also covers infinite-dimensional manifolds, such as spaces of curves or maps, and the singularities of smooth maps.

The methods combine the analysis of elliptic operators, the calculus of variations and algebraic topology. Many results equate an analytic quantity, such as the number of independent solutions of an equation, with a topological one that can be computed without solving anything. The area is tied to differential geometry and partial differential equations, and to physics through gauge theory and spectral problems.

Critical points on a torus

Stand a torus upright like a tire and let hhh be the height of each point. The function hhh has four critical points: a minimum at the bottom, two saddles at the bottom and top of the hole, and a maximum at the top. The alternating count 1−2+1=01 - 2 + 1 = 01−2+1=0 is the Euler characteristic of the torus. On a sphere, height has one minimum and one maximum, and 1+1=21 + 1 = 21+1=2.

Central objects

Smooth manifold
A space with local coordinates in which functions can be differentiated.
Differential form
An object that can be integrated over curves, surfaces or higher-dimensional pieces of a manifold.
Elliptic operator
A differential operator, like the Laplacian, whose highest-order part is invertible in every nonzero direction.
Laplace-Beltrami operator
The generalization of the Laplacian to functions on a Riemannian manifold.
Morse function
A smooth function all of whose critical points are nondegenerate.
Fredholm operator
A bounded linear operator with finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel; its index is their difference in dimension.
Singularity of a map
A point where the derivative of a smooth map fails to have maximal rank.

Landmark theorems

  1. Stokes' theorem

    If MMM is a compact oriented smooth nnn-manifold with boundary and ω\omegaω is a smooth (n−1)(n-1)(n−1)-form on MMM, then ∫Mdω=∫∂Mω\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M} \omega∫M​dω=∫∂M​ω.

  2. De Rham's theorem1931

    For a smooth manifold MMM, integration of differential forms over smooth chains induces an isomorphism between the de Rham cohomology of MMM and its singular cohomology with real coefficients.

  3. Sard's theorem1942

    The set of critical values of a C∞C^\inftyC∞ map between smooth manifolds has measure zero.

  4. Morse inequalities

    Let fff be a Morse function on a closed manifold MMM with ckc_kck​ critical points of index kkk, and let bkb_kbk​ be the Betti numbers of MMM. Then ck≥bkc_k \ge b_kck​≥bk​ for every kkk, and ∑k(−1)kck=χ(M)\sum_k (-1)^k c_k = \chi(M)∑k​(−1)kck​=χ(M).

  5. Hodge theorem

    On a compact oriented Riemannian manifold, every de Rham cohomology class contains exactly one harmonic form.

  6. Atiyah-Singer index theorem1963

    For an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold without boundary, the analytic index, which is the dimension of the kernel minus the dimension of the cokernel, equals the topological index, which is computed from the symbol of the operator and characteristic classes of the manifold.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Hearing the shape of a convex drum

    Is a smooth convex planar domain determined, up to rigid motion, by the Dirichlet eigenvalues of its Laplacian?

  • Pólya's conjecture

    Is the kkk-th Dirichlet eigenvalue of every bounded Euclidean domain at least the leading term in Weyl's law? It is proved for tiling domains and for balls.
