33 Special functions

Special functions

Which functions beyond sine and logarithm turn up so often that they earned names of their own?

Special functions are the particular functions that arise again and again in mathematics and physics: the gamma function, Bessel functions, Legendre and other orthogonal polynomials, hypergeometric functions, elliptic functions and many more. Most appear as solutions of specific differential or difference equations, or as integrals that cannot be expressed through elementary functions. The area catalogs their properties and the relations among them.

Typical results are identities, integral representations, recurrence relations, asymptotic expansions and addition formulas. Much of the structure is explained by symmetry: many special functions are matrix entries of group representations or solutions singled out by separation of variables. The subject serves number theory, combinatorics, probability and mathematical physics, and its qqq-analogs link it to partitions and quantum groups.

Hypergeometric series
A power series in which the ratio of consecutive coefficients is a rational function of the index.

Orthogonal polynomials
A sequence of polynomials, one of each degree, mutually orthogonal for a given weight or measure.
Elliptic function
A meromorphic function on the complex plane with two independent periods.
Theta function
A rapidly converging series in exponentials of quadratic terms, from which elliptic functions can be built.
