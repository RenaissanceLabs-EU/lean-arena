52 Convex and discrete geometry

Convex geometry

How densely can equal balls be packed, and what shapes can a polyhedron take?

Convex geometry studies convex sets, those containing the whole segment between any two of their points, along with their volumes, surface areas, sections and projections. Discrete geometry studies arrangements of finitely or countably many geometric objects: packings of balls, tilings of space, configurations of points and lines, and polytopes, the higher-dimensional versions of polygons and polyhedra. Typical questions are extremal: which arrangement is densest, smallest or most numerous?

The subject mixes geometric inequalities, combinatorial counting and linear algebra, with input from analysis, topology and probability, and in recent decades from computer-assisted proof. Convex bodies in high dimensions show behavior that links the field to functional analysis. Polytopes tie it to linear programming and algebraic geometry, lattices tie it to number theory, and its combinatorial side underlies computational geometry.

Counting on a polyhedron

A cube has V=8V=8V=8 vertices, E=12E=12E=12 edges and F=6F=6F=6 faces, and V−E+F=2V-E+F=2V−E+F=2. An octahedron gives 6−12+8=26-12+8=26−12+8=2 and a dodecahedron 20−30+12=220-30+12=220−30+12=2. Euler's formula says this holds for every convex polyhedron. A quick consequence: every face has at least three edges and every edge lies on two faces, so 3F≤2E3F\le2E3F≤2E, which gives E≤3V−6E\le3V-6E≤3V−6. A convex polyhedron with 6 vertices has at most 12 edges.

What to notice
  • Teal faces come from the cube's faces and red faces from the cut corners. The cube's faces shrink to points as the red ones grow to fill the surface.
  • Vertices, edges and faces change, but V − E + F is always 2, as for every convex polyhedron.
  • The octahedron has a vertex at the center of each face of the cube, and a face for each corner. Cube and octahedron swap their numbers of vertices and faces.

Central objects

Convex set
A set that contains the segment joining any two of its points.
Convex hull
The smallest convex set containing a given set.
Polytope
The convex hull of finitely many points; in three dimensions, a convex polyhedron.
Lattice
The set of all integer combinations of a basis of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn.
Packing
An arrangement of copies of a body whose interiors do not overlap.
Tiling
A covering of space by bodies whose interiors do not overlap.
Mixed volume
The coefficients of the polynomial giving the volume of a weighted sum of convex bodies.

Landmark theorems

  1. Euler's polyhedron formula

    For every convex polyhedron in three-dimensional space with VVV vertices, EEE edges and FFF faces, V−E+F=2V-E+F=2V−E+F=2.

  2. Minkowski's convex body theorem

    Let Λ\LambdaΛ be a lattice in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn and KKK a convex set symmetric about the origin with volume greater than 2ndet⁡Λ2^n\det\Lambda2ndetΛ. Then KKK contains a point of Λ\LambdaΛ other than the origin.

  3. Brunn-Minkowski inequality

    For nonempty compact sets A,B⊂RnA,B\subset\mathbb{R}^nA,B⊂Rn with A+B={a+b}A+B=\{a+b\}A+B={a+b}, vol(A+B)1/n≥vol(A)1/n+vol(B)1/n\mathrm{vol}(A+B)^{1/n}\ge\mathrm{vol}(A)^{1/n}+\mathrm{vol}(B)^{1/n}vol(A+B)1/n≥vol(A)1/n+vol(B)1/n.

  4. Helly's theorem

    Let a finite family of at least d+1d+1d+1 convex sets in Rd\mathbb{R}^dRd be given. If every d+1d+1d+1 of them have a common point, then all of them have a common point.

  5. Upper bound theorem1970

    Among all convex polytopes of dimension ddd with nnn vertices, the cyclic polytope has the largest number of faces of every dimension.

  6. Kepler conjecture2005

    No packing of equal balls in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3 has density greater than π/18≈0.7405\pi/\sqrt{18}\approx0.7405π/18​≈0.7405, the density of the face-centered cubic packing. The proof was announced in 1998 and published in 2005.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Densest sphere packings

    The greatest density of a packing of equal balls is known only in dimensions 1, 2, 3, 8 and 24.

  • Mahler's conjecture

    Among convex bodies symmetric about the origin, is the product of the volumes of the body and its polar smallest for the cube? This is proved up to dimension 3.

  • Hadwiger's covering conjecture

    Can every convex body in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn be covered by 2n2^n2n smaller homothetic copies of itself? This is open for n≥3n\ge3n≥3.
