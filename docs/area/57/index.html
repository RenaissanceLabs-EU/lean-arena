57 Manifolds and cell complexes

Topology of manifolds

Can you tell a knotted loop from an unknotted one, or a sphere from a doughnut, without cutting anything?

A manifold is a space that looks, near each of its points, like ordinary Euclidean space of some dimension. Curves and surfaces are the first examples, and there are analogs in every higher dimension. This area classifies manifolds and studies how they are assembled from simple pieces such as cells, simplices and handles. It includes knot theory and the topology of dimensions three and four.

The main tools are invariants such as the fundamental group, homology, intersection forms and knot polynomials, together with cut-and-paste techniques such as surgery and handle decompositions. In dimensions five and above, classification questions reduce largely to algebra. Dimensions three and four behave differently and draw on hyperbolic geometry, geometric flows and gauge theory from physics.

Counting on a surface

Divide a surface into polygons and compute V−E+FV - E + FV−E+F, vertices minus edges plus faces. A cube gives 8−12+6=28 - 12 + 6 = 28−12+6=2, and so does every other division of a sphere. A torus cut into a 3 by 3 grid of squares has 9 vertices, 18 edges and 9 faces, giving 9−18+9=09 - 18 + 9 = 09−18+9=0. The number depends only on the surface, so a sphere cannot be deformed into a torus.

Central objects

Manifold
A space that near every point looks like Euclidean space of a fixed dimension.
Knot
A closed loop in three-dimensional space, studied up to deformation that never passes the loop through itself.
CW complex
A space built by attaching cells of increasing dimension along their boundaries.
Handle decomposition
A way of building a manifold by attaching thickened cells called handles, one at a time.
Cobordism
A manifold whose boundary consists of two given manifolds, relating one to the other.
Smooth structure
A choice of coordinate charts on a manifold that makes differentiation meaningful.
Intersection form
The integer bilinear form on the middle homology of a 4-manifold that counts intersections of surfaces.

Landmark theorems

  1. Classification of closed surfaces

    Every closed connected surface is homeomorphic to exactly one of the following: the sphere, a connected sum of g≥1g \ge 1g≥1 tori, or a connected sum of k≥1k \ge 1k≥1 real projective planes.

  2. Milnor's exotic spheres1956

    There exist smooth manifolds that are homeomorphic to the 7-sphere S7S^7S7 but not diffeomorphic to it.

  3. h-cobordism theorem1962

    Let WWW be a compact smooth manifold of dimension at least 6 whose boundary is the disjoint union of MMM and NNN, with WWW, MMM, NNN simply connected and both inclusions M→WM \to WM→W, N→WN \to WN→W homotopy equivalences. Then WWW is diffeomorphic to M×[0,1]M \times [0,1]M×[0,1].

  4. Freedman's classification theorem1982

    Two closed simply connected topological 4-manifolds are homeomorphic if and only if they have isomorphic intersection forms and equal Kirby-Siebenmann invariants. In particular, a closed 4-manifold homotopy equivalent to the 4-sphere is homeomorphic to it.

  5. Donaldson's diagonalization theorem1983

    If the intersection form of a closed, simply connected, smooth 4-manifold is positive or negative definite, then it is diagonalizable over the integers.

  6. Poincaré conjecture2003

    Every closed simply connected 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere.

Open problems and frontiers

  • Smooth 4-dimensional Poincaré conjecture

    Is every smooth 4-manifold that is homeomorphic to the 4-sphere also diffeomorphic to it?

  • Slice-ribbon conjecture

    Is every knot that bounds a smooth disk in the 4-ball a ribbon knot?

  • Jones polynomial and the unknot

    Is the unknot the only knot whose Jones polynomial equals 1?
